Our guide for your site

Measure the wind before you decide

Wind depends heavily on the exact spot. A few months of readings on a pole at the planned height will tell you if a turbine is worth buying. Meanwhile, a solar and battery system can go ahead and be expanded later.

Ask Redbay Energy about my site

This is a first guide, not an assessment. Wind varies from one spot to the next, so we confirm on site before recommending a turbine.

How it works

Two sources, one battery bank

  • Solar panels

    Do most of the work in daylight, and work almost anywhere in Kenya.

  • A wind turbine on a mast

    Adds power in the evening, at night and in stormy weather, when the sun is weak. It needs a windy, open site.

  • Charge controllers and batteries

    Each source charges the batteries safely, and the batteries carry you through the night.

  • An inverter

    Turns battery power into normal household power. A grid connection can be added where net metering applies.

Is wind worth it in my area?

+

It depends on your exact site, not just the county. Open, high ground, ridges, lakeshores and parts of the coast tend to be windier; sheltered plots are not. Use the check on this page, then we confirm on site.

Can I put a small turbine on my roof?

+

We do not recommend it. Wind near a roof is turbulent, and vibration can carry into the building. A turbine on its own mast, clear of trees and roofs, is the better choice.

Which turbines do you work with?

+

Redbay Energy installs Elege vertical-axis turbines, chosen for sites where the wind changes direction often. They suit gusty sites; on steady, strong wind, other types can be more efficient, and we say so if your site needs one.

What if my site has little wind?

+

Then more solar and batteries is usually the better buy, and we will say so. A hybrid only makes sense when the wind is there.

Who is a hybrid system for?

+

Boarding schools, homes off the grid, farms and places with frequent blackouts, where power is needed at night and the site is windy.
