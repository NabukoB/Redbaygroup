Waterproofing methods compared: which one do you need?

Torch-on membranes, liquid membranes, cementitious coatings and crystalline treatments compared: where each works best on roofs, bathrooms, tanks and basements.

By Redbay Group · 22 November 2026

Waterproofing methods

There is no single best method

Each waterproofing system suits different places. The right choice depends on what the area is, whether people walk on it, whether it will be tiled or exposed to the sun, and whether water pushes from the outside or sits on top.

Torch-on bituminous membranes

Thick sheets bonded to the surface with heat, with the joints overlapped and sealed. They are tough and suit large flat roofs. On roofs exposed to the sun, a mineral-finished or protected membrane lasts longer. They need skilled installers, as poor joints are where they fail.

Liquid-applied membranes

Brushed, rolled or sprayed on to form a seamless coat with no joints. They are good for balconies, terraces and roofs with many corners, pipes and upstands. The thickness must be built up properly in several coats.

Cementitious coatings

Cement-based coatings mixed with special additives and applied like a thick paint. They bond well to concrete and masonry and can be tiled over, which makes them a common choice for bathrooms, wet areas and water tanks.

Crystalline treatments

Applied to concrete, they react with moisture to form crystals that block the pores and fine cracks. They work from either side, which makes them useful for basements, retaining walls and tanks where water pushes from outside.

Quick guide

  • Exposed flat roof: torch-on or liquid membrane
  • Roof terrace or balcony to be tiled: liquid membrane or cementitious coating under the tiles
  • Bathroom and shower: cementitious coating or liquid membrane under the tiles
  • Water tank: cementitious or crystalline system suitable for water storage
  • Basement or retaining wall: crystalline treatment or external membrane
