How to check a building contractor before you hire

Simple checks to make before handing over your project and money: licence, past work, insurance and how the contractor quotes.

By Redbay Group · 2 November 2026

Check the licence

Building contractors in Kenya must be registered with the National Construction Authority (NCA). Ask for the registration certificate and check the name and category on the NCA's website. The category tells you the size of project the contractor is registered for.

See real work

Photos are a start, but go further:

  • Ask for two or three past clients you can call
  • Visit a site that is under construction, not only finished houses
  • Look at the quality of walls, corners and finishes up close

Ask about insurance

Contractor's All Risk insurance covers damage to the works during construction, for example from fire, theft or storms. Ask whether the contractor carries it for your project.

Read the quotation carefully

A clear, itemised bill of quantities is a good sign. It shows the contractor has measured your project, and it makes comparing quotes fair. Be careful with very low lump-sum quotes; missing items usually come back as extra costs.

Agree it in writing

Before work starts, have a written agreement that covers:

  • The full scope of work and the drawings it follows
  • Payment stages tied to finished work, not to dates
  • The timeline, and what happens if it slips
  • Who buys which materials

Questions to ask past clients

When you call a past client, ask about the things that go wrong on most sites:

  • Did the work finish on the date promised? If not, how late, and why?
  • Did the final cost match the quotation? What was added, and was it explained?
  • Was the contractor easy to reach during the work, and did they come when they said they would?
  • Were there defects after handover, and how quickly were they fixed?
  • Would you hire them again?

Warning signs

Be careful when a contractor:

  • Asks for a large payment upfront before buying any materials or starting any work
  • Cannot show an NCA certificate, or shows one in a different name
  • Gives a lump-sum price with no breakdown and will not explain it
  • Pushes you to sign quickly, or says the price is only valid today
  • Has no address, no past sites you can visit and no one you can call
  • Does not want the agreement written down

Stay involved while the work is going on

Checking the contractor does not end when you sign. Visit the site regularly or send someone you trust. Ask for dated photos at each stage, especially of work that will be covered, such as foundations, steel and pipes. Keep copies of the drawings, the agreement, every payment and every change you agree to. If you live far from the site, an independent inspection before each payment is a sensible way to make sure you pay only for finished work.
