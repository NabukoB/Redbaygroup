Flat roof leaking? Causes and lasting fixes

Why concrete flat roofs leak in Kenya, how to find where water gets in, and the repairs and waterproofing that stop leaks for good, not just until the rains.

By Redbay Group · 22 November 2026

Why flat roofs leak

A concrete slab on its own is not waterproof. Most flat roof leaks come from one of these:

  • No waterproofing membrane, or one that has cracked, blistered or lifted with age
  • Too little fall, so rain sits in puddles instead of draining away
  • Blocked or too few outlets, so water backs up after heavy rain
  • Weak details where the roof meets parapet walls, pipes and water tank stands
  • Cracks in the slab from movement, heat or overloading

Find where the water gets in

Water often travels inside the slab before it shows on the ceiling, so the stain below is not always under the leak. Check the roof after rain: look for standing water, cracks, lifted membrane and damage around outlets, parapets and pipes. A controlled flood test on one area at a time can confirm the source.

Patching rarely lasts

Sealant on a crack or a coat of paint on the roof may stop a leak for a season, but the water usually finds another way in. A lasting fix deals with drainage and the whole waterproofing layer, with extra care at corners, upstands and outlets, which is where most leaks start.

A lasting repair

A proper flat roof repair usually involves:

  • Clearing and repairing outlets, and adding more if needed
  • Correcting the fall with a screed so water drains to the outlets
  • Repairing cracks and preparing a clean, sound surface
  • Applying a membrane suited to how the roof is used, turned up the parapet walls
  • Reinforcing corners, joints and pipe penetrations
  • Protecting the membrane if people will walk on it or it will be tiled

Look after it

Clear outlets and gutters before each rainy season, keep heavy items off the membrane, and check the roof after storms. Small problems found early are cheap to fix.
